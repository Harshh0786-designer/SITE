About us

The story is the car.

Car Story exists because the used-car trade had the priorities backwards — a lot of cars, and very little known about any of them. We hold fewer, and we know all of it.

How it started

One bay in a Lower Parel mill compound, in 2016, and a car nobody else wanted to take on: a Continental GT with a folder of receipts and no story tying them together. It took six weeks to work out what had actually happened to that car. By the end of it we understood the business we wanted to be in.

Not volume. Not turning stock in a fortnight. The slow, unglamorous work of establishing what a car is — and then being able to prove it.

What we believe

A car you cannot explain is a car you should not sell. If we cannot account for a panel, a service gap or a warning light, the car does not go on the floor. Several have gone back out of the gate for exactly that reason.

Faults are not embarrassing; hiding them is. Every car we list carries its blemishes in writing — a resprayed door, a set of tyres near the end, two track days at Buddh. The price already reflects them, so there is nothing to discover later.

The person who inspects the car is the person who fixes it. Nothing is subcontracted out, because a technician who knows they will be doing the work does not write a convenient inspection.

Why the name

Every car arrives with a story already written — an owner who drove it properly or one who let it sit, a winter it spent outdoors, a repair someone chose to do well. Most of the trade spends its energy erasing that story. We spend ours reading it, writing it down, and handing it to you with the keys.

How a car reaches the floor

Six steps, in this order, with no shortcuts through the middle. A car that fails at any of them does not reach the floor.

  • Sourced

    Usually from an owner we already know, or from a marque specialist we have bought from before. We do not buy at auction.

  • Bought outright

    We take ownership before we take a photograph. Nothing on our floor belongs to someone else.

  • Stripped back

    Wheels off, undertrays out, interior panels away. Around forty hours on an older car, and longer if what we find asks for it.

  • Rectified

    Factory-supplied parts against the chassis number, fitted here. Every invoice stays with the car.

  • Driven

    A few hundred kilometres of real roads. Hybrids go on a fortnightly charge cycle; folding roofs get cycled thirty times.

  • Written up

    Graded across five areas, priced against what the inspection found, and published with the faults included.

The workshop

What is in it

Four bays and a two-post lift, in a mill compound off Senapati Bapat Marg. Paint depth gauges, marque diagnostic hardware, a leak-down tester and a hydraulic pressure rig — the last one because McLaren suspension cannot be judged any other way.

It is a working floor rather than a showroom, which is why viewings are by appointment. Book ahead and the car is warmed, out of the bay and ready to drive when you arrive.

Who is in it

A small team of technicians, two of whom came from marque dealerships and one from a restoration shop. They inspect the cars, they do the work, and they sign the grade that goes on the page.

If you ask a question about a car on this site, the answer comes from someone who has had their hands inside it.
