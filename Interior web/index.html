01 — The house

We design the inside of a building as if the outside were watching.

Meridian House was drawn around one fact: at 18:40 in late summer, the sun drops into a gap in the tree line due west of the site. Every decision after that — the twenty-four foot glass span, the hearth set off-axis, the corridor that runs the long way round — was made to protect that twelve-minute window.

Altus works in that register. Interior architecture, joinery, light and the objects that live between them, handled as one continuous piece of work rather than a sequence of trades.

03 — Services

What we actually do.

Eight disciplines, one drawing set. We take a project from the first site walk to the day the art goes on the wall.

  • Interior architecture

    Plan, section and circulation. We move walls, floor levels and openings before we ever choose a fabric.

    Drawings · 1:50 / 1:20

  • Custom millwork

    Every case piece drawn to the millimetre and cut by a shop we have used for eleven years. The entry hall wardrobe wall is one of ours.

    Shop drawings · prototypes

  • Lighting design

    Layered circuits, warm-dim sources, and linear coves that disappear into the beam line. Nothing over 2700K after dark.

    DALI · scene programming

  • Material & finish palettes

    Physical boards, not renders. Stone is selected at the slab yard; timber is chosen by board before it is cut.

    Sample boards · site mock-ups

  • Furniture & fittings

    Full FF&E: specification, procurement, custom commissions, and the logistics of getting a twelve-foot sectional up a meadow track.

    Schedules · procurement

  • Terrace & threshold

    Where the inside stops. Paving falls, fire tables, planting immediately against the glass, and the pool coping detail.

    With landscape architect

  • Art & object curation

    We buy for the room, not the résumé. Commissioned ceramics, found driftwood, and one sculpture that had to arrive by crane.

    Commission · placement

  • Project delivery

    Site attendance, trade coordination, snagging and handover. One director stays on the job from first sketch to final walkthrough.

    Weekly site · handover file

04 — Process

Six stages, in this order.

The sequence matters. Nothing is specified before the site has told us what it wants, and nothing is ordered before it has been drawn.

  1. 01

    Site reading

    A full day on the ground at three different hours. Sun path, wind, the noise of the road, where the land actually drains.

    1 week
  2. 02

    Concept & massing

    Plans, sections and a physical model. We argue about circulation now so we are not arguing about it on site.

    4–6 weeks
  3. 03

    Material board

    Stone, timber, metal and textile in your hands, in the real light of the real room. Signed off before anything is detailed.

    3 weeks
  4. 04

    Documentation

    The full set: joinery, lighting, finishes schedule, FF&E. This is where a project is either saved or lost.

    8–12 weeks
  5. 05

    Making

    Shop visits, site attendance, mock-ups on the wall. We answer the trades in hours, not weeks.

    Build duration
  6. 06

    Handover

    Styling, art placement, lighting scenes programmed, and a maintenance file that tells you how to look after the stone in ten years.

    2 weeks
